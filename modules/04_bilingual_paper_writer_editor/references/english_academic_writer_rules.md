# English Academic Writing Rules & De-AI-ing (Nature & IEEE Standards)
# اصول نگارش و ویرایش مقالات انگلیسی به سبک مجلات معتبر بیومکانیک

Writing in international biomechanics journals (*Journal of Biomechanics*, *IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering*, *Gait & Posture*) demands precision, conciseness, and active scholarly voice. AI-generated English papers exhibit distinct stylistic fingerprints that reviewers immediately identify and penalize.

---

## 1. The English AI Blacklist (Banned Words & Cliches)

| AI Cliche / Tell (❌ Avoid) | Scholarly Alternative (✅ Use) | Why Reviewers Object |
|---|---|---|
| **Delve into / shed light on** | **Investigate, examine, quantify** | Metaphorical fluff; use concrete experimental verbs. |
| **A testament to / a pivotal role** | **Contributes significantly to, increases** | Inflation of importance without numerical evidence. |
| **Crucial, vital, paramount, profound** | **Required, necessary, critical** | Overused dramatic adjectives; let the effect size speak. |
| **In today's fast-paced world / modern era** | **State the physiological problem directly** | Formulaic throat-clearing; journals reject historical preambles. |
| **Furthermore, moreover, additionally (spam)** | **Logical transitions, coordinating conjunctions** | Connecting every sentence with formal adverbs creates robotic rhythm. |
| **It is worth noting that / it should be mentioned** | **State the fact directly** | Preamble announcement; deletes without loss of meaning. |
| **Not only ... but also ...** | **Direct compound sentence** | Parallelism often abused to inflate dual observations. |
| **Harnessing / leveraging / utilizing** | **Using, applying** | Corporate tech jargon; "use" is almost always superior. |
| **Comprehensive, holistic, nuanced** | **Describe the exact parameters analyzed** | Vague praise words used to pad weak methodology. |

## 2. Eliminate Nominalization (Verb Smothering)

Nominalization turns lively action verbs into heavy Latinate nouns accompanied by weak verbs (make, perform, conduct):

| ❌ Weak Nominalization | ✅ Direct Active Verb |
|---|---|
| Conducted an investigation into joint kinetics | **Investigated joint kinetics** |
| Performed an evaluation of muscle activation | **Evaluated muscle activation** |
| Made an estimation of the center of pressure | **Estimated the center of pressure** |
| Provided a demonstration of stability | **Demonstrated stability** |
| Came to the conclusion that | **Concluded that** |

---

## 3. Active vs. Passive Voice Calibration

- **Methods Section:** Passive voice is conventional and appropriate when the actor is irrelevant:
  - *Correct:* "Kinematic trajectories were low-pass filtered using a 4th-order zero-lag Butterworth filter."
- **Introduction, Findings & Discussion:** First-person plural ("we") and active verbs are preferred in modern high-impact journals:
  - *Weak:* "It was observed that knee flexion was increased by the exoskeleton."
  - *Strong:* "The adaptive exoskeleton increased stance knee flexion by $14.2\%$ ($p = 0.004$)."
  - *Weak:* "In this study, a new method is proposed."
  - *Strong:* "We propose a quasi-stiffness control law..."

---

## 4. Precision in Scientific Hedging

Never overstate or understate the certainty of your findings:

| Verb | Strength Level | Proper Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **Proves** | Absolute ($100\%$) | **Forbidden in empirical biomechanics** (experiments confirm hypotheses, they do not prove them). |
| **Demonstrates / Shows** | High certainty | Use when statistical significance is robust ($p < 0.01$, large effect size) and model assumptions hold. |
| **Indicates / Suggests** | Moderate certainty | Use when a mechanistic trend is evident but sample size or confounding variables require caution. |
| **May / Might / Could** | Speculative | Use in the Discussion section when postulating unmeasured physiological mechanisms (e.g., motor unit recruitment). |

---

## 5. Quantitative Precision & Statistical Reporting
- Always report exact $p$-values to 2 or 3 decimal places (e.g., $p = 0.024$), never bare statements like "results were significant".
- When $p < 0.001$, report $p < 0.001$ (never $p = 0.000$).
- Pair every $p$-value with a measure of effect size (e.g., Cohen's $d$, partial $\eta^2$) and sample variability (Mean $\pm$ SD or $95\%$ Confidence Interval $[CI]$).
